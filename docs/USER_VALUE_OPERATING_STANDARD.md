# User Value Operating Standard

Hired AI exists to produce better career and hiring outcomes with less friction. Technical superiority is required when it helps create that result; it is not the end goal by itself.

## Decision rule

Before prioritizing a capability, ask:

1. What outcome is this user actually trying to achieve?
2. Does this capability move them materially closer to it?
3. Does it increase success probability, reduce effort, save time, improve trust, increase income, improve retention, or create compounding future advantage?
4. Can Hired AI prepare or execute more of the work so the user does not have to operate another tool?
5. Does the action require evidence, consent, authorization, a professional gate, or a legal constraint?
6. What verified outcome should teach Hired AI whether the intervention worked?

Features that cannot answer those questions should not outrank higher-value work merely because they are technically sophisticated.

## Candidate standard

Candidates want stronger work, better pay, less wasted effort, credible positioning, stronger interviews, a realistic path into the career they want, and continuity across the journey. Maya should proactively identify the next useful move, close the smallest high-value gap, choose the strongest opportunity-access route, coordinate work inside one conversation, and learn from the candidate's verified outcomes.

## Gig-worker standard

Gig workers want more paid utilization, better clients, repeat business, less dead time, portable proof and more economic control. Hired AI should help turn verified gig outcomes into reusable career evidence, reduce platform concentration when appropriate, increase income, and support transitions into independent business ownership or salaried work when the user wants them.

## Employer and startup standard

Employers want the right person faster, less screening waste, stronger evidence of capability, fewer bad hires and less recruiting overhead. Startups should not need a recruiting department before they can hire well. Hired AI should turn roles into evidence requirements, produce explainable shortlists, coordinate evaluation, use job-relevant assessments where appropriate and minimize administrative work.

## Institution standard

Training programs, schools, nonprofits and workforce organizations need participants to convert training into employment. Hired AI should connect proof to opportunity access, preserve participant consent and measure placement, retention and advancement so institutions can improve what actually changes careers.

## Near-zero-friction rule

The user should experience one continuous Career OS. Hired AI may contain many engines, but the user should not have to manually operate them. Prepare first. Ask only for missing facts or required authorization. Never remove consent, evidence, professional requirements or truth controls merely to reduce clicks.

## Outcome-learning rule

Outcome data exists to improve the product and the user's future decisions. Relevant outcomes include opportunity usefulness, relationship usefulness, application conversion, interview conversion, offers, compensation change, transition success, time saved, post-hire satisfaction, retention, promotion, mobility and gig-income progression.

Use verified, privacy-protected, consented data according to the existing career-insight and proprietary-model gates. Do not turn competitive research into customer-facing competitor identity or public comparative claims unless the founder explicitly asks for that product behavior.
