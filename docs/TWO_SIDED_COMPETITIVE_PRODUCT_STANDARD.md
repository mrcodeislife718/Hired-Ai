# Hired AI Two-Sided Competitive Product Standard

Last benchmark review: 2026-09-08.

This document is an internal product and engineering standard, not a marketing superiority claim. Competitor capabilities change. Public competitor materials should be rechecked before making external comparisons.

## Product position

Hired AI is one coordinated system with two first-class customer sides:

- **Candidate / employee side:** help a person understand and document their career, discover opportunities, become more competitive, apply selectively, interview well, negotiate, secure strong work, advance, and preserve future career options.
- **Employer side:** help an organization define the real work, find consented evidence-backed talent, test job-relevant capability, run structured interviews, collaborate, make better hiring decisions, close candidates, and learn from hiring outcomes.

Maya is the conversational operating surface on both sides. Candidate Maya and employer Maya may share language, reasoning primitives and marketplace intelligence, but they must not share private state across the marketplace without explicit authorization.

## Competitive lessons to absorb

### Conversational recruiting agents

Useful pattern:
- conversational intake rather than long forms;
- deep role/candidate understanding;
- fast matching;
- interview and salary guidance;
- employer hiring-brief calibration;
- curated introductions;
- outcome-based commercial options.

Hired AI response:
- retain conversational depth;
- build durable Candidate Career Twins and Employer Hiring Twins rather than one-time intake summaries;
- turn candidate intake into synchronized career documentation and action;
- turn employer intake into an owned role, pipeline, scorecard, assessment and outcome system;
- keep candidate profile sharing deny-by-default and organization-aware;
- combine marketplace matches with authorized external sources so network size is not the only route to value.

### Human recruiter marketplaces

Useful pattern:
- specialist judgment;
- passive-talent relationships;
- nuance in ambiguous searches;
- human persuasion and closing.

Hired AI response:
- automate repeatable research, evidence mapping, workflow and follow-up;
- preserve human escalation for ambiguous/high-stakes evaluation and closing;
- support human recruiters as users/partners rather than pretending all human judgment is obsolete;
- retain evidence and outcome history so human judgment becomes auditable and reusable.

### AI-native ATS and recruiting systems

Useful pattern:
- employer-owned pipeline;
- workflow control;
- custom stages and assessments;
- proprietary organization data;
- integrations and operational visibility.

Hired AI response:
- employers keep durable organization/role/pipeline state;
- Maya can provide hands-off recommendations without turning the employer into a consumer of opaque delivered lists;
- preserve RBAC, auditability, tenant isolation, export and integration boundaries;
- add conversational operation on top of the owned system to reduce setup and recruiter-management burden.

### Large professional networks

Useful pattern:
- enormous talent reach;
- identity and career graph;
- recruiter search filters;
- outreach and messaging;
- candidate recommendations;
- ATS integrations;
- hiring collaboration and analytics.

Hired AI response:
- use authorized external networks/connectors to augment first-party marketplace density;
- move beyond Boolean/profile similarity into attributable requirement-to-evidence reasoning;
- reduce recruiter search labor through conversational sourcing and continuous matching;
- avoid spam incentives by optimizing qualified conversations and durable matches rather than message volume;
- preserve transparent organic ranking separate from paid promotion.

## Weaknesses Hired AI must structurally avoid

### Opaque candidate sharing
Candidate profiles are private unless the candidate deliberately changes visibility. Allowed and blocked organizations, compensation sharing and preference sharing remain explicit controls. No paid employer tier may bypass candidate consent.

### Delivered-shortlist dependency
An employer owns the hiring workspace: roles, requirements, candidate stages, scorecards, assessments, interview evidence, decisions, outcome history and exports. Maya may deliver a shortlist, but the employer is not locked into a black-box recruiting service.

### Small-network dependence
The marketplace should compound first-party density, but sourcing architecture must also support authorized ATS, job, portfolio, credential, talent-pool, professional-network and other connectors. Hired AI value must not collapse when a niche is sparse in the first-party network.

### Keyword and AI-application inflation
Requirements map to attributable evidence. Verified assessments, work samples, credentials, references and other legitimate sources can strengthen confidence. A polished resume or AI-written application cannot silently become proof of capability.

### Unstructured or proxy-heavy screening
Use real job outcomes, role-relevant capability evidence, structured interviews and job-relevant assessments. Challenge requirements that are not genuine hard gates. Separate facts from inference and retain fairness audit evidence.

### Automation overreach
Maya may research, calibrate, prepare, recommend and coordinate under policy. Candidate applications/outreach and consequential employer reject/offer/hire actions remain governed by authorization boundaries appropriate to the action.

### Paid ranking corruption
Subscriptions, seats, usage fees, success fees and promoted distribution may buy capabilities, service, workflow and clearly labeled reach. They cannot buy a better organic candidate fit score, readiness score, employer-quality score or fabricated evidence.

### Platform lock-in
Core user facts and employer hiring state remain exportable. Paid plans buy additional capability; they do not become ownership of the underlying career or hiring facts.

## Free versus paid law

Free must be useful enough to prove the product works. Paid must make the user materially more capable, faster, broader, better coordinated or better supported.

Trust, truth and safety are not premium features.

Always free on the candidate side:
- conversational onboarding;
- Career Twin/core career record;
- core career documentation including master resume foundation;
- opportunity discovery/matching foundation;
- role-readiness/evidence explanation;
- basic interview practice;
- opportunity/application tracking;
- visibility/privacy/export/deletion controls.

Paid candidate capability can add:
- synchronized target-role document portfolios;
- deeper employer/compensation intelligence;
- professional-presence/networking intelligence;
- advanced competitive-selection analysis;
- governed acquisition orchestration and follow-up;
- advanced interview simulation;
- negotiation and funnel/outcome intelligence;
- human review and high-stakes service.

Always free on the employer side:
- conversational role intake;
- role calibration/Hiring Twin foundation;
- owned basic hiring pipeline;
- consent-aware candidate preview;
- structured scorecard/interview kit;
- evidence/ranking explanations;
- core data ownership/export and permissions.

Paid employer capability can add:
- expanded sourcing and direct introductions;
- multiple active hiring operations;
- verified assessments;
- team collaboration;
- AI interviewing/custom assessment automation;
- continuous sourcing/watch automation;
- scheduling/pipeline automation;
- private talent pools;
- hiring outcome analytics;
- ATS/HRIS/identity integrations;
- enterprise SSO/RBAC/audit/SLAs;
- internal mobility/workforce intelligence;
- enterprise APIs/private controls.

## Candidate success loop

conversation → Career Twin → evidence discovery → career documents → opportunity discovery → role-specific evidence mapping → selective application → interview preparation → offer/negotiation → employment → 30/90/365 outcome learning → better future decisions

The system should help the candidate secure the best realistic career outcome, not maximize applications.

## Employer success loop

conversation → Hiring Twin/role calibration → sourced consented candidates → evidence map → job-relevant assessment where useful → structured interviews → human hiring decision → offer/close → hire → quality/retention outcome learning → better future role definitions and selection

The system should help the employer secure top talent for the actual work, not maximize candidate volume.

## Two-sided marketplace law

A successful match requires both sides to win.

A candidate who can perform but will predictably reject the compensation or working conditions is not a strong match. An employer that wants the candidate but presents poor verified working conditions is not automatically a recommendation. A candidate who wants the role but lacks a mandatory legal credential cannot be positioned around the gate. An employer willing to pay more cannot purchase a false fit signal.

Hired AI should optimize for durable, mutually successful matches using attributable evidence, explicit preferences, consent, calibrated uncertainty and observed outcomes.
