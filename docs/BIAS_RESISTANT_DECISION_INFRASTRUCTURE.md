# Bias-Resistant Hiring Decision Infrastructure

Hired AI / Maya treats hiring quality as an evidence problem rather than a prestige or continuity problem.

Canonical decision chain:

`job requirement → capability → evidence → confidence → decision → later outcome`

The system must never infer race, religion, disability, health condition, sexuality, age, or other protected/sensitive characteristics to perform this analysis. It examines decision logic, proxy use, evidence quality, and later outcomes instead.

## Candidate Risk Map

Before application or reentry work, Maya identifies weak-proxy exposure such as layoffs, employment gaps, non-mandatory degree screens, non-linear career changes, caregiving, self-employment, early-career status, and unconventional backgrounds. The mitigation strategy is to keep context factual and proportionate while strengthening direct role-relevant proof.

## Evidence Substitution Engine

Nonessential tenure and pedigree requirements can be challenged when verified evidence directly demonstrates the capability they are intended to predict. Legal, licensing, authorization, safety, and genuinely mandatory credential requirements remain hard gates and cannot be substituted away.

## Structured Interview Builder

Employer-side interviews are generated from role capabilities and success outcomes. Questions ask for specific examples, candidate-owned actions, observable results, and verifiable detail. Strong / acceptable / weak evidence anchors reduce inconsistent gut-feel evaluation.

## Fact / Inference Audit

Consequential judgments are represented as observed fact, inference, supporting evidence, and confidence. Unsupported inferences are challenged rather than silently promoted into truth.

## Rejection Reason Quality Check

A rejection rationale must identify a specific missing capability or hard gate and connect it to evidence. Vague reasons such as “not enough experience” are challenged when direct evidence already demonstrates the capability supposedly represented by the tenure proxy.

## Counterfactual Candidate Review

The system can test whether a decision changes when only a weak proxy changes. Example: would the same job-relevant evidence be evaluated differently if it came from a candidate without an employment gap? A changed decision triggers review of the proxy’s actual job-relevant purpose.

## Blind Evidence Review

Where appropriate, initial evidence review can remove unnecessary identity and pedigree fields while retaining work samples, assessments, outcomes, projects, skills, references, and other relevant evidence. This is an evidence-review mechanism, not a replacement for lawful identity verification where required later.

## Candidate Recovery Mode

Rejection is not automatically interpreted as proof of candidate failure. Maya classifies available evidence into: unknown, evidence gap, presentation problem, role mismatch, process issue, possible proxy risk, or genuine qualification gap. Strategy changes follow the classification instead of reacting blindly to every rejection.

## Hiring Quality Feedback Loop

Hired AI can associate hiring signals with 30 / 90 / 365-day performance and satisfaction outcomes. This makes it possible to learn which screens and evidence types actually correlate with successful hires and which merely appear respectable. Small samples and missing data remain visible rather than being overclaimed.

## Fairness Audit Trail

Employer decision tools record actor, action, requirement where relevant, fact, inference, evidence identifiers, confidence, decision, and rationale. This gives recruiters and hiring teams an explainable record of why a criterion was accepted, challenged, or used.

## Product invariant

The system protects candidates from unsupported inference while protecting employers from false-negative hiring decisions. Fairness is implemented as better evidence discipline and decision quality, not as a hidden demographic score.
